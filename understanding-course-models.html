Understanding Grading Models

What are Grading Models?

Grading Models are the framework used to measure each student's growth and competency with a course. In every class students will get grades for their assignments and contributions to class; the grading model is the structure that assembles those grades into a full picture.

Not every score you receive is as important, or as reflective of your ability and understanding, so the scores need to be organised to mirror the effort each student puts into each application and the course's goals for its students.

Grading Models apply #SystemMapping to respond to the explanatory challenge: "What course content and skills has the student learnt?" The different models use different mappings to answer that question.

Do you know what Grading Model your class uses?

If you navigate to Forum → Outcome Index → Courses → View Course Score Calculation, you will see either:

Assessment Type Model

click here to skip to the CO Based Model

If your Forum looks like this photo on the left, with assignment and in-class score categories, you are being graded within the Assessment Based Model.

The Assessment Based Model divides the system according to each score's #Medium. The medium of a poll, of an essay (assignment), of an interview (assignment), of class participation.

It prescribes a fixed importance to the #Medium.

[Explanation]

Sometimes students can be misled early in the semester when they've only received a couple of in-class scores, which are accounting for most of the total grade. Even after the first assignment is returned, in-class scores are likely to account for most of the student's grade. Don't worry! Early in the semester, it's important to try your best and trust the process.

The Assessment Based Model makes it easy for the student to see the importance of each assignment.

Assessment Type Model shows a clear linear trajectory: assignments are almost always assigned an increasing share of the final grade over the course of the semester as expectations for the students' learning rise.

CO Based Model

If your Forum looks like this photo on the right, with LOs and HCs arranged within Course Outcomes, you are being graded within the CO Based Model.

The CO Based Model divides the system according to each score's #Purpose. Scores are always associated with an HC or LO; in the CO Based Model we organise all HCs/LOs together according to their underlying purpose, the reason the course is teaching those concepts or skills.

It prescribes a fixed importance to the #Purposes, but does not strictly define how much each assessment will impact your final grade.

In the CO Based Model, assignments contribute to each CO's "bin", like in-class scores do, but they are more impactful because:

  1. you receive many scores
  1. an assignment score might be "multiplied", or "added to the bin" many times, according to the weight of the assignment, written as 2× to say "score added twice".

Sometimes grades can look weird when the student has received few scores within an important course outcome. If it's early in the semester, as always, try your best and trust the process, but within the CO Based Model the student can change this more directly by intentionally applying an LO from that CO in class to seek out more feedback and scores within that category.

Some students find the CO Based Model tricky to understand and predict, because the impact of the scores from early in the semester will be changed by the scores received after. Unlike in the Assessment Type Model, where a share of your final grade is completely fixed after you submit an assignment, you never "finish" contributing to any category within the CO system map. Although that might make it tricky to understand, it gives students the power to "pick" how impactful they want their assignment to be by tagging more HCs/LOs.
