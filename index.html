Understanding Grading Models

What are Grading Models?

Grading Models are the framework used to measure each student's growth and competency in a course. In every class, students get grades for their assignments and contributions to class; the grading model is the structure that assembles those grades into a full picture.

Not every score you receive is equally important, or equally reflective of your ability and understanding, so scores need to be organised to mirror the effort each student puts into each application and the course's goals for its students.

Grading Models apply #SystemMapping to respond to the explanatory challenge: “What course content and skills has the student learnt?” The different models use different mappings to answer that question.

Do you know what Grading Model your class uses?

If you navigate to Forum → Outcome Index → Courses → View Course Score Calculation, you will see either:

Assessment Type Model

Click here to skip to the CO Based Model

If your Forum looks like this photo on the left, with assignment and in-class score categories, you are being graded within the Assessment Type Model.

The Assessment Type Model divides the system according to each score's #Medium: for example, a poll, an essay (assignment), an interview (assignment), or class participation.

It prescribes a fixed importance to the #Medium.

Each assessment type, which is the unit of division for this system mapping, maps to one ‘bin’. All scores from that assessment type fill its bin.

The more scores in a bin, the less any individual score is worth.

Scores from important assessments, with wide bins, will have a larger impact on the grade.

The Assessment Type Model makes it easy for the student to see the importance of each assignment.

The Assessment Type Model shows a clear, linear trajectory: assignments are almost always assigned an increasing share of the final grade over the course of the semester as expectations for the students' learning rise.

Sometimes students can be misled early in the semester when they've only received a couple of in-class scores, which account for most of the total grade. Even after the first assignment is returned, in-class scores are likely to account for most of the student's grade. Don't worry! Early in the semester, it's important to try your best and trust the process.

CO Based Model

If your Forum looks like this photo on the right, with LOs and HCs arranged within Course Outcomes, you are being graded within the CO Based Model.

The CO Based Model divides the system according to each score's #Purpose. Scores are always associated with an HC or LO.

In the CO Based Model, we organise all HCs/LOs together according to their underlying purpose, the reason the course is teaching those concepts or skills.

It prescribes a fixed importance to each #Purpose, but does not strictly define how much each assessment will impact your final grade.

In the CO Based Model, assignment scores contribute to each CO's “bin” just like in-class scores do.

But assignments represent much more work and care from each student, so they should be more representative of a student's ability.

So assignments are given ‘weights’. The weight of an assignment “multiplies” each score from the assignment, or “adds the score to the bin” many times.

This is written as ‘2×’ to say “score added twice”.

Sometimes grades can look weird when the student has received few scores within an important course outcome. If it's early in the semester, as always, try your best and trust the process, but within the CO Based Model the student can change this more directly by intentionally applying an LO from that CO in class to seek out more feedback and scores within that category.

Some students find the CO Based Model tricky to understand and predict, because the impact of the scores from early in the semester will be changed by the scores received after. Unlike in the Assessment Type Model, where a share of your final grade is completely fixed after you submit an assignment, you never “finish” contributing to any category within the CO system map. Although that might make it tricky to understand, it gives students the power to “pick” how impactful they want their assignment to be by tagging more HCs/LOs.
