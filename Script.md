# Understanding Grading Models (`understanding-original.html`, `understanding.html`)

## Title page

**Title (heading):** Understanding Grading Models

## What are Grading Models? (text on the left, floating scores on the right)

**Heading:** What are Grading Models?

**Step — floating scores:**
> Grading Models are the framework used to measure each student's growth and competency in a course. In every class, students get grades for their assignments and contributions to class; the grading model is the structure that assembles those grades into a full picture.

**Step — floating scores:**
> Not every score you receive is equally important, or equally reflective of your ability and understanding, so scores need to be organised to mirror the effort each student puts into each application and the course's goals for its students.

**Step — scores grouped into circles:**
> Grading Models apply #SystemMapping to respond to the explanatory challenge: “What course content and skills has the student learnt?” The different models use different mappings to answer that question.

## Assessment Type Model

**Heading:** Assessment Type Model
**Skip link:** Click here to skip to the CO Based Model

**Step — the left screenshot stays:**
> If your Forum looks like this photo on the left, with assignment and in-class score categories, you are being graded within the **Assessment Type Model**.

**Step — circles (Polls, Class Comments, Essay, Research Paper, Final Assignment):**

**Step — fixed importance (the circles line up in a row, in the order of their bins):**
> It prescribes a fixed importance to the #Medium.

**Step — the diagram (bins appear behind the circles and the scores move in; the weight bars can be dragged):**
>

**Step — back to default:**
> The Assessment Type Model makes it easy for the student to see the importance of each assignment.

**Step — trajectory staircase (scores stacked in timeline order, assignments in single columns, shares marked on the right):**
> The Assessment Type Model shows a clear, linear trajectory: assignments are almost always assigned an increasing share of the final grade over the course of the semester as expectations for the students' learning rise.

**Step — early in the semester (the diagram beside its stack: assignments still to come left out and named above, a couple of low in-class scores standing tall):**
> Sometimes students can be misled early in the semester when they've only received a couple of in-class scores, which account for most of the total grade. Even after the first assignment is returned, in-class scores are likely to account for most of the student's grade. Don't worry! Early in the semester, it's important to try your best and trust the process.

## CO Based Model

**Heading:** CO Based Model

**Step — the right screenshot:**
> If your Forum looks like this photo on the right, with LOs and HCs arranged within Course Outcomes, you are being graded within the **CO Based Model**.

**Step — circles regroup (CO 1, CO 2):**
> The CO Based Model divides the system according to each score's #Purpose. Scores are always associated with an HC or LO; in the CO Based Model we organise all HCs/LOs together according to their underlying purpose, the reason the course is teaching those concepts or skills.

**Step — fixed importance (the circles line up in a row, in the order of their bins):**
> It prescribes a fixed importance to the #Purposes, but does not strictly define how much each assessment will impact your final grade.

**Step — the diagram (bins appear behind the circles and the scores move in; the weight bar can be dragged):**
> In the CO Based Model, assignments contribute to each CO's "bin", like in-class scores do, but they are more impactful because:
> 1. you receive many scores

**Step — multiplied scores:**
> 2. an assignment score might be "multiplied", or "added to the bin" many times, according to the weight of the assignment, written as 2× to say "score added twice".

**Step — sparse CO (a couple of low scores in one bin):**
> Sometimes grades can look weird when the student has received few scores within an important course outcome. If it's early in the semester, as always, try your best and trust the process, but within the CO Based Model the student can change this more directly by intentionally applying an LO from that CO in class to seek out more feedback and scores within that category.

**Step — how to visualise this:**
> Some students find the CO Based Model tricky to understand and predict, because the impact of the scores from early in the semester will be changed by the scores received after. Unlike in the Assessment Type Model, where a share of your final grade is completely fixed after you submit an assignment, you never “finish” contributing to any category within the CO system map. Although that might make it tricky to understand, it gives students the power to “pick” how impactful they want their assignment to be by tagging more HCs/LOs.

## Demonstration — Assessment Type Model sidebar (`calculations-original.html`)

**The Example**
> You receive scores in the same way, regardless of the course model.

**Sorted by Assessment Type**
> Not all applications of a skill demonstrate the student's ability as well as another, so scores need to be categorised and given different power over the final grade.
>
> In the Assessment Type Model, scores are categorised by the time and place (the assessment) where the HC or LO was applied.

**In-Class and Assignments**
> The assessment categories are organised into either in-class scores or assignment scores, but this categorisation does **not** affect how much they are worth. They are only grouped this way for #organization.
>
> Each assignment and in-class assessment type is responsible for a share of the final grade.

**The Grade as an Area**
> If your final grade is represented by this box, each assessment type has been given an area within it that is definite and fixed.

**Assignments Subdivided**
> When you have not yet finished the course, the areas that have been assigned to future assessments are completely 'cut away'. Your overall grade to date would be represented by the sub-area of whatever you have completed.

**Filled With Scores**
> Each area gets filled by its scores, which means more scores (tagging more LOs) will 'dilute' the impact of each score.

## Demonstration — CO Model sidebar (`calculations-original.html`)

**The Example**
> You receive scores in the same way, regardless of the course model.

**Weights are applied**
> Assignment scores are multiplied according to the associated assignment weight. An application of an LO in a 1× class poll will have half as much impact as an application in a 2× assignment.

**Sorted by Learning Outcome**
> Not all applications of a skill demonstrate the student's ability as well as another, so scores need to be categorised and given different power over the final grade.
>
> In the CO Based Model, scores are categorised by LO and HC, but they are categorised again before the grouping is assigned its share of the final grade.

**LOs Form Course Outcomes**
> The LOs (and sometimes HCs) all belong to a Course Outcome, which is the scale of analysis where score importance is dictated.
>
> Each CO is responsible for a share of the final grade.

**The Grade as Bins**
> Each CO is a bin you will keep filling up all semester. If you have no scores in a CO, that bin is cut away completely from your grade to date.
