# Minerva University Branding and Visualisation Guide

- Do group related data for clearer structure and meaning.
- Do use color purposefully to distinguish categories without clutter.
- Do label directly on the chart instead of relying on long legends.
- Do summarize key insights with grouped totals or annotations.
- Do keep the layout clean and balanced — avoid overlap and crowding.
- Do provide clear titles and axis labels with proper units.
- Do prioritize readability through good contrast and font hierarchy.
- Do remove unnecessary visuals — keep only what adds value.


- Don’t overload the chart — limit the number of variables or lines shown (i.e., don’t violate the limited processing capacity #communicationdesign principle).
- Don’t use too many similar colors — guarantee clear visual distinction.
- Don’t crowd the legend — keep it concise and readable.
- Don’t include irrelevant or zero-value data — filter out noise.
- Don’t make axes unclear — label units and context clearly.
- Don’t let lines or elements overlap excessively — avoid clutter.
- Don’t mix unrelated variables — group by meaningful categories.
- Don’t use vague titles — clearly state what the visualization shows.
